Simple technology for older adults: what to consider

Before choosing a device, try it with the person who will use it. Their comfort, preferences, and choices are the starting point.

By the SELMI Team · Published September 16, 2026 · Updated October 6, 2026 · 4-minute read

A tool is simple when it helps with a specific task without adding unnecessary steps. The best choice depends on the person, what they enjoy, and the support they want to receive.

1. Start with an activity they enjoy

Talking with family, listening to music, or checking a schedule are good starting points. Ask what they would like to do and what difficulties they currently face. Avoid choosing features they do not need.

2. Try the screen and sound at home

Check whether they can read the text, distinguish buttons, and understand the voice at a comfortable volume. Look at the screen under the room's usual lighting. Try it with the person and let them explore at their own pace.

3. Review the steps for everyday tasks

Try starting a call, returning to the home screen, and correcting a selection. Important actions should be easy to find, and it should be possible to leave a screen without getting lost.

4. Understand what information is shared

Ask what data is stored, who can see it, and how to change permissions or request deletion. Agree with the person on what they want to share with the family.

5. Ask about connectivity, support, and cost

  • Does it need a permanent WiFi connection?
  • What happens if the internet or power goes out?
  • Who helps with installation and questions?
  • What is the total price, and what does it include?
  • How do you return the device or cancel the service?
A useful trial: ask them to show you how they would do their favorite activity. Notice what's clear and what needs explaining; adjust the choice together.

Technology as part of everyday support

A device can add convenience and shared moments. Human contact and respect for the person's decisions remain central.
